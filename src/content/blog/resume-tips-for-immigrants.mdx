# Resume Tips for Immigrants: How to Stand Out to Canadian Employers

Your resume is often the first thing a Canadian employer sees about you. Getting it right is critical — and for immigrants, there are specific adjustments that can make a big difference.

## The Core Principles of a Canadian Resume

### Keep it Concise

Canadian resumes are typically **1–2 pages**. Recruiters spend an average of 7 seconds on a first scan. Every word counts.

### Lead with Impact

Start each bullet point with a strong action verb and follow it with measurable results:

- ❌ "Responsible for managing a team"
- ✅ "Led a cross-functional team of 8 engineers to deliver a $2M project 2 weeks ahead of schedule"

### Remove Personal Information

Do NOT include:
- Photo
- Date of birth
- Marital status
- Immigration status

This information is protected under Canadian human rights legislation, and including it can actually hurt your application.

## Structuring Your Resume

### 1. Contact Information
Name, phone (Canadian number), email, LinkedIn URL, city and province only (no full address needed).

### 2. Professional Summary (Optional but Powerful)
A 2–3 sentence snapshot of who you are and what you bring. Tailor this for every application.

### 3. Work Experience
List in reverse chronological order. For each role, include:
- Job title, company name, city/country, dates
- 3–5 bullet points focused on achievements and impact

### 4. Education
Degree, institution, year. If your degree is from outside Canada, you may want to note it has been assessed by WES or equivalent.

### 5. Skills
Technical skills, software proficiencies, certifications. Keep this relevant to the job.

## ATS Optimization

Most companies use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to filter resumes before a human ever reads them. To get through:

- **Use keywords from the job posting** verbatim
- Avoid tables, graphics, and unusual fonts (they confuse ATS)
- Submit as a Word document or ATS-friendly PDF

## Addressing International Experience

Don't hide or minimize your international experience — it's a strength. Frame it clearly:

- Include the country in the company location
- Briefly explain the company's size/industry if it's not well-known in Canada
- Highlight skills and outcomes that are universal
