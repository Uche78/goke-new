# How to Get Your First Job in Canada as a New Immigrant

Moving to Canada is an exciting milestone — but the job search can feel overwhelming. The good news is that thousands of immigrants find great careers in Canada every year, and with the right approach, you can too.

## 1. Understand the Canadian Job Market

Canada's job market has some important differences from what you may be used to:

- **Networking is everything.** Over 70% of jobs in Canada are filled through referrals and networking, not job boards alone.
- **Canadian experience is valued** — but not always required. Focus on highlighting transferable skills.
- **Soft skills matter.** Canadian employers look for communication, teamwork, and adaptability alongside technical skills.

## 2. Adapt Your Resume for Canadian Standards

Your resume should be:

- **1–2 pages maximum** (unlike some countries where longer CVs are expected)
- **No photo, date of birth, or marital status** (this is illegal to request in Canada)
- **Achievement-focused** — use numbers and results wherever possible ("Increased sales by 25%")
- **ATS-optimized** — include keywords from the job posting

## 3. Build Your Canadian Network

Even before you land your first job, start building your network:

- Join LinkedIn and connect with people in your industry in Canada
- Attend local immigrant settlement programs and job fairs
- Join professional associations related to your field
- Connect with other immigrants who've made the transition

## 4. Get Your Credentials Recognized

If your profession is regulated in Canada (medicine, engineering, nursing, etc.), you'll need to get your credentials assessed. Contact the relevant regulatory body for your province.

For non-regulated occupations, an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) from WES or similar organizations can strengthen your applications.

## 6. Be Patient and Persistent

Your first Canadian job may not be your dream job — and that's okay. Many successful immigrants started in entry-level positions to build local experience and networks before advancing to roles that matched their true qualifications.

Stay consistent, keep learning, and use every opportunity to grow your Canadian experience.
